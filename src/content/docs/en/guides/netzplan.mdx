Beside the [network documentation](/en/guides/netzwerk-dokumentation/) and the [racks](/en/guides/racks/), the **Network** group holds:

- **Overview:** figures on pages, networks, VLANs, zones, racks and cables.
- **Topology:** the devices and their connections as a map.
- **Networks and VLANs:** IP networks, VLANs, zones, the communication matrix and links to the outside.
- **Cabling:** every cable of the racks with both ends.

All of it is part of the Community edition. The **dependencies** in the same group are a feature from Pro.

## Rights

- Every role may read; networks, VLANs and zones hold no personal data.
- **Administrator**, **Information security** and **Editing** may change them when bound to no location (see [Roles and rights](/en/reference/rollen/)).
- The **occupancy** of a network and the **LLDP neighbours** in the topology are shown only to roles that may read the discovery's findings.

## Networks, VLANs and zones

<Steps>

1. Open **Network › Networks and VLANs**, view **Zones**, and create your zones, such as clients, servers, DMZ, management and guests, each with its **protection needs**.

2. In the view **VLANs**, create VLANs with **number** (1 to 4094), **name**, **zone** and **location**. A number exists once per location.

3. In the view **Networks**, create the IP networks: **address range (CIDR)** such as `192.168.10.0/24` or `2001:db8:10::/64`, **name**, **VLAN**, **zone**, **location**, **gateway** and **DHCP from** and **DHCP to**. Gateway and DHCP range must lie in the network.

</Steps>

A network counts as **public** when it is reachable on the internet and overlaps no range IANA sets aside; a wide range such as `10.0.0.0/7` is therefore not public. The [NIS2 registration](/en/pro/nis2/) takes the public networks over at the press of a button.

A zone with VLANs, networks or allowed connections cannot be deleted, nor a VLAN with networks. When a location is deleted, its VLANs and networks stay without a location; the audit log says so.

### VLANs on the switches

When the [collector](/en/guides/collector/#snmpv3-and-lldp) reads switches over SNMPv3, the view **VLANs** shows the card **VLANs on the switches** below the list: each VLAN number the switches report, with the name they give it, the switches, the number of ports untagged and tagged and the VLAN of the network plan with the same number. If it is missing there, **Take over** opens the form with number and name filled in; you add zone and location yourself. Nothing is taken over by itself.

Only roles that may read the collectors' findings see the card.

## Occupancy of a network

Clicking the range opens the network. The card **Occupancy** shows:

- how many addresses are in use and the share, with a warning from 90 %,
- the next free addresses outside the DHCP range, without the gateway,
- the addresses in use with device, MAC address and source: **Agent** (the device's agent reports it) or **Network scan** (a collector found it).

The occupancy is computed when the network is opened, from what agents report and collectors find, and is not stored. For IPv6 networks SentryAssets shows the addresses seen only.

## Communication matrix

In the view **Communication matrix** you record which zone may talk to which, over which **services** (such as HTTPS, SSH, DNS) and for which **reason**. This is the target: it supports the segmentation under NET.1.1 and NIS2 and is the basis for reviewing the firewall rules. SentryAssets does not configure a firewall.

## Topology

**Network › Topology** draws the devices and their connections as a map:

- **cables** between two devices in the racks (solid), also across patch panels; the connection then names the patch panels it passes,
- **LLDP neighbours** the switches report over SNMP (dotted),
- **links by hand** from the network plan, such as the internet connection at the firewall (dashed).

The ends outside the network stand at the top, below them each device a row under its nearest neighbour. Clicking a device opens the asset. With **Location** you show only the devices of a location and their direct neighbours. Below the map all connections stand as a list.

**Copy as Mermaid diagram** puts the map as text on the clipboard; on a page of the network documentation, **Diagram from topology** inserts it straight into the editor.

<Aside>
A way across patch panels counts when it reaches a device at both ends; how it runs shows the [path at the port](/en/guides/racks/#patch-panels-front-rear-and-path). Connections to devices you may not see stay out.
</Aside>

## Cabling

**Network › Cabling** lists every cable of the racks with ID, type, colour and length and both ends: rack, location, device or patch panel and port, for a patch panel with its face. The search finds cable, rack, device and port, such as `Gi1/0/24`. A cable with one end only shows as **open**.

## References from the documentation

On a page of the network documentation, **Reference** also inserts networks, VLANs and zones. They always show the current name, and a network's page names the pages that mention it.
