Stolen or guessed passwords are the most common way into other people's accounts. A second factor bound to a device or a key makes a captured password worthless on its own. IT-Grundschutz asks for strong authentication above all where accounts are reachable from the internet or hold special rights (module ORP.4).

## What matters

- **The risky ways in first:** administration, remote access, cloud services, then everyone.
- **Phishing-resistant methods** such as FIDO2 keys or sign-ins bound to the device also protect against fake sign-in pages.
- **Closing gaps:** switch off legacy protocols that bypass multi-factor sign-in; plan and watch break-glass accounts.
- **Evidence:** the policy and a report of which accounts still lack a second factor.
